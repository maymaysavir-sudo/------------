# Storytelling Formats for Short-Form Content

**Source**: https://www.instagram.com/p/DUoiEXNkshc/ (also: https://www.instagram.com/reel/DaVHo_ClOm_/ - the full 6-step blank-page process below; also: https://www.instagram.com/reel/DayOnBMhbL-/ - the bad/good/great execution tiers below)
**Category**: content-strategy

## Core Insight
Great short-form storytelling uses one specific story type per video, follows a defined structure, and pairs with the right format and visuals, it's not just "telling what happened."

## How It Works
- Focus on **one story per video** using one of the 7 main short-form storytelling types
- Follow the **hook → value → CTA** structure
- Stories should follow formal storytelling structures (setup, conflict, resolution)
- **Tell** what happened OR **show** what happened, don't mix randomly
- Visuals: either no visuals (pure delivery), or add lots, past images, B-roll, clips
- Film in one of the **3 formats best for storytelling shorts** (voiceover, multitasking, shot/angle change)
- Keep stories **broad** enough for the video length; don't overcrowd with detail

## Blank Page to a 1M-View Storytelling Short - 6-Step Process (companion reel)
- **Step 1 - Choose a main storytelling type**: start from the 7 storytelling types; write story ideas under each, then pick one.
- **Step 2 - Use a proven script structure**: each of the 7 types has one or more structures. Build your own by: find a viral storytelling video that follows a structure -> transcribe it -> templatize into a fill-in-the-blank template usable for any niche -> save it in Google Docs.
- **Step 3 - Write your script**: using your template, write it line by line in bullets.
- **Step 4 - Choose a format**: pick one of the 3 best for storytelling shorts (voiceover / shot-angle change / audio + b-roll + text) and adjust the script to fit.
- **Step 5 - Plan visuals**: script in bullets -> add the shots to film -> add editing notes for the b-roll images/videos that show the moment being told -> finished shootable script.
- **Step 6 - Film, edit, post.**
- Example persona used in the reel: a fake fitness coach "Katniss Everdeen who lost over 50 pounds".

## Bad / Good / Great Execution Tiers (companion reel)
Same story, three quality tiers across four execution choices:
- **Camera/format**: bad = talking directly to camera the whole video; good = talking while multitasking; great = voiceover while past videos/images from the time period play.
- **Script**: bad = pre-styling (memorizing/scripting word-for-word); good = sculpting your video (loose structure while filming); great = sculpting with a proven storytelling structure.
- **Emotion delivery**: bad = just telling your emotions (stating them); good = just showing your emotions (visibly displaying them); great = making the viewer feel your emotions (the story itself evokes the feeling, not just you performing it).
- **Hook source**: bad = coming up with your own hook; good = using a proven viral verbal hook; great = using a proven viral hook across written, verbal, and visual simultaneously.

## When to Use
For any "me content" video, personal story, founder story, wins & losses, lessons, weekly diary. Match the storytelling type to the goal (connection, authority, relatability). Use the bad/good/great tiers as a pre-publish checklist to upgrade camera work, script prep, emotional delivery, and hook sourcing one tier at a time.
